Truth targets: what the truth graph gives, and what an ideal clustering could separate

How to read this page: every term and number explained ↓

No reconstruction, truth graph only. Left: the truth graph, from the gun particle to the base particles (◆, one colour each). Middle: the same in 3D, trajectories and each deposit coloured by the base particle with most energy in it. Right: the targets, one colour each.

Base particles: every particle of the truth graph that crossed into the calorimeter (it has its own boundary crossing), owning its deposits and those of whatever it produced inside. Every crossing counts, also below another one: an electron that radiated in the tracker and its brem photons and conversion pairs that crossed on their own are separate base particles. (The graph's caloBoundary level keeps only the outermost of nested crossings, so it would give the electron all of them.)

Targets (ideal-clustering test): give every cell (layer cluster or rechit), whole, to the object with the most energy in it. An object passes if that ideal cluster holds more than of its energy (completeness) and more than of the truth energy in it is its own (purity): what the metrics call reconstructed individually, for the best clustering any algorithm could make. An object that fails cannot be separated by any algorithm working on these cells, so it is merged with the object it is most mixed with, and the test is repeated until every object passes. A target that is one particle keeps its colour. The test is run separately on layer clusters and on rechits, so the targets (and their colours) can differ between the two.

How to read this page

The chain, from truth to score

  1. Truth graph (left pane): the generated particle and everything the simulation saved below it, with the process that made each particle (hover a node).
  2. Base particles (◆): every particle of the truth graph that crossed into the calorimeter. Each one owns its own calorimeter deposits and those of whatever it produced inside (the simulation does not save those). Middle pane: each deposit in the colour of the base particle with most energy in it.
  3. Targets: base particles an ideal clustering can reconstruct on their own, from the separability test. Base particles that cannot be separated are merged into one target (e.g. a soft photon lying inside a shower, or pileup inside the signal). Right pane, "targets".
  4. Truth groups: what the clustering is scored against. Signal targets joined by the tracksters, when a trackster holds most of each and they overlap (more than half of the smaller one lies in layer clusters shared with the other, e.g. a brem photon showering along its electron). Most groups are one target.
  5. Score: C and P of the emulated CLUEstering tracksters against the truth groups (panel "Tuning objectives"; right pane, "scoring of one truth group"; the energy ledger when a group is focused).

Energies (GeV)

at the boundary
the true energy of a base particle when it crossed into the calorimeter (simulation). For a hadron it is well above its energy in layer clusters: the layer clusters are on the electromagnetic scale, and part of a hadron's energy is never seen (nuclear binding, neutrons).
in layer clusters / in rechits
reconstructed energy: each rechit's energy shared between the particles in proportion to their simulated energy in that cell; only layer clusters the trackster-building step may use. This is the energy every score uses.
target E, group E
the sum of that energy over the base particles of the target, over the targets of the group. "Pileup inside" is the part from pileup particles that could not be separated from the signal: it is truth, not contamination.
in Kg
the part of the group's energy in its ideal cluster Kg: the layer clusters in which the group has more energy than any other object. The most any clustering of whole layer clusters can collect.
in other objects' layer clusters
group E − in Kg: energy of the group in layer clusters led by something else. No clustering of whole layer clusters can collect it without taking more of the other object: not counted against C.
free contamination
energy in Kg that is not the group's (other particles, noise): no clustering can avoid it, so it does not count against P.
trackster Ek
the reconstructed energy of a trackster: the sum of its layer clusters (times the fraction of each it gets). For a trackster of a group it splits exactly into collected + group outside Kg + free + avoidable.
collected
the group's energy in its tracksters, in layer clusters of Kg (green).
group outside Kg
the group's energy its tracksters took from layer clusters led by others: not credited, not penalised.
avoidable contamination
everything in the group's tracksters that is not the group's, in layer clusters outside Kg (red): other showers, pileup, noise-only layer clusters. A better clustering could have left it out.
missed
layer clusters of Kg in none of the group's tracksters (amber): unclustered, or in another trackster.

Scores

C (completeness)
collected / in Kg, summed over scored groups. 1 = the tracksters hold all the group's energy that an ideal clustering could.
P (purity)
1 − avoidable / trackster energy, over the tracksters of scored groups. 1 = nothing in them that a better clustering could have left out.
F
how many tracksters a group is split into, energy weighted (1 = one trackster). Reported, not tuned: the trackster linking joins pieces later.
scored group
a group holding a signal target (more than 50% signal energy) of at least 2 GeV. Pileup groups are never scored; they only matter when a scored group's tracksters take them in (avoidable contamination).
ideal completeness / ideal purity
in Kg / group E and in Kg / everything in Kg: how much an ideal clustering collects and how pure it is. Context, not a score: the ideal clustering scores C = P = 1.
separability test: completeness, purity of a target
the test that makes targets: the target's ideal cluster holds more than 50% of it (completeness) and it is more than 50% of the truth energy there (purity). Not C and P.
signal in pileup targets / unscored
signal energy that ended in a target that is mostly pileup, or in groups with no target of 2 GeV.

With pileup

The targets and scores are computed on the whole event. The page shows the cells within ΔR < 0.5 of each signal particle, the truth graph of the signal interaction (tick "pileup" for the pileup particles in the cone), and greys objects below 1 GeV. Colours: the 20 most visible objects of each endcap have their own colour; beyond that colours repeat only far apart. The same colour never means the same object.
